# Version 1 Training Target Logic Memo

## 1. Purpose of the Training Targets

The training targets should help the user do three things:

1.  turn test results into usable workout guidance
2.  choose sensible power targets for common session types
3.  see where their strengths and weaknesses sit across durations

The targets are not there to diagnose physiology. They are there to make
the profile actionable.

## 2. Best Framing for the Outputs

Use a hybrid of duration-based targets and workout categories.

Do not make classic zone charts the main system.

## 3. Recommended Training Target Categories

-   Short Power Repeats
-   Medium Hard Intervals
-   Sustained Hard Intervals
-   Threshold-Style Sustained Work
-   Profile-Based Strengths and Limiters

## 4. Recommended Predicted Durations

30s, 45s, 60s, 2m, 3m, 4m, 6m, 8m, 10m, 12m, 15m, 20m

## 5. How Targets Should Be Presented

-   Quick Summary
-   Workout Target Cards
-   Duration Table
-   Interpretation Notes

## 6. What the App Can Safely Imply

-   Practical starting points for training
-   Relative strengths across durations

## 7. What the App Must Not Imply

-   Exact physiological zones
-   Threshold detection
-   Perfect precision across all durations

## 8. Suggested User-Facing Language

Use targets as starting points, not exact prescriptions.

## 9. Version 1 Restraint Rules

-   No full zone systems
-   No automatic programming
-   No recovery prescriptions

## 10. Final Recommendation

Use a duration-based, category-driven system grounded in tested values.
